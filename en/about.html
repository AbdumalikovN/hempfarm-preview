The cluster that brought desert land back into use

HEMPFARM JV LLC is a joint venture: an industrial hemp agro-industrial cluster established by Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 511 of 20 September 2022. Full cycle, from cultivation to export.

Cabinet Resolution No. 511 Licence No. 108836 Major investment project
Project status

Backed by a resolution — built on site

Resolution No. 511 sets the goal of “attracting foreign direct investment, phased development of unused land, production of high value-added goods and creation of new jobs in rural areas”.

01 · Investment

Major investment in the region

Infrastructure, a greenhouse and processing lines have been built — investment in Kiziltepa District, which previously had no similar production facilities.

02 · Land

350+ ha developed and expanding

Land that had been out of agricultural use for more than 30 years is now irrigated and receives mineral fertilizer; the cluster's land bank is 1,015 ha.

03 · Export

High value-added products

2025 — first export shipment (China); 2026 — export contract with a buyer in India. Fiber parameters are confirmed by testing.

04 · People

Rural jobs

About 50 permanent jobs and 120–150 seasonal jobs, filled daily for about 8 months a year — in Kiziltepa District, where this land previously provided no jobs.

Why hemp

It was not displaced. It was banned.

Millennia, a break and a return: the story of a material coming back to the market.

5000 BC · beginnings

One of humanity's first fibers

In Asia, hemp was used for weaving and papermaking thousands of years BC.

It is neither a new crop nor a fashionable one — it is one of the oldest crops humans ever learned to cultivate.

7,000 yearsof hemp fiber
15th–19th centuries · Age of Sail

The word canvas comes from the word cannabis

Entire fleets relied on hemp: ropes, rigging, sails, hull caulking.

The ships that discovered America sailed under hemp canvas. Maritime powers regarded this fiber as a strategic raw material and stockpiled it like weapons.

canvas ← cannabissailcloth — named after the plant
1937 · the break

Hemp did not lose out to competition. It was removed from the market.

Two reasons, and neither had anything to do with fiber quality.

The 1937 US tax ban and the arrival of cheap petrochemicals — nylon and synthetics from the 1930s, wood pulp in paper. World hemp fiber production fell from more than 300,000 tonnes in 1961 to about 75,000 by the early 1990s.

300,000 t → 75,000 tworld fiber production · a fourfold drop
2018 · the turnaround

Both reasons are gone

The US made industrial hemp legal again, and Uzbekistan issued its first licences in 2022 — the ban is being lifted country by country.

And petrochemicals are becoming more expensive for the first time in a century: carbon reporting, microplastics, raw-material origin requirements.

2018 · 2022US · Uzbekistan — a legal framework for industrial hemp
2024 · standard

It is already in the building code

Hempcrete has been added as a separate appendix to the US International Residential Code.

Carmakers are switching interior panels to natural-fiber composites. This is no longer a trend — it is a standard, and standards are not reversed.

IRC 2024Appendix BL · hempcrete
Today · market

The market is growing faster than plants are being built

The industry's bottleneck is neither demand nor raw material, but processing.

Fiber accounts for 41% of the market; textiles are the fastest-growing application.

Mission

To turn unused land into a source of world-class sustainable materials, creating jobs and added value in Uzbekistan.

Strategy

To establish full-cycle production — from the field to finished fiber and hurd products — and bring “Made in Uzbekistan” products to the global market.

Values

Transparency and licensed operations, independently verified quality, long-term relationships with partners and care for the land.

Sustainability

Sustainability built into the model

Industrial hemp yields a harvest on saline, low-organic soils where traditional crops are not economically viable — about 7 t of biomass per hectare per season. No pesticides are used.

01

Carbon sequestration

8–15 t of CO₂ per hectare per growing cycle — about twice as efficient as forest (industry data); a supporting argument for partners' ESG reporting.

02

Land restoration

Degraded saline soils have been returned to productive, licensed use.

03

Crop rotation — planned

Rotating hemp with wheat is planned to improve soil fertility.

04

Water savings and zero waste

3–4 times less water than cotton (industry data); 100% of the stalk is used — both the fiber and the hurd become products.
