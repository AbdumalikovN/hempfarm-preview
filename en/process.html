From seed to export: one integrated cycle

All stages at one site in Navoi Region: cultivation, harvesting, retting, decortication, deep processing, quality control and shipment. Click a stage to see the details.

Production chain

Six stages of one cycle

Stage 01 of 06

Cultivation

Licensed varieties with THC < 0.2%, grown under precision center-pivot irrigation.

The crop is Cannabis Sativa, industrial hemp. The land in Kiziltepa District, low in organic matter and saline in places, was prepared for three years: mineral nutrition, irrigation and agrochemical monitoring. Industrial hemp yields a harvest where traditional crops are unprofitable, and it is grown without pesticides. Every year before harvest, the competent authorities test the crop for THC content.

Sowing4 Valley systemsno pesticides
Stage 02 of 06

Harvesting

2–3 harvests a year, about 7 t of biomass per hectare per season.

The dry, sunny climate makes it possible to harvest 2–3 crops a year, while most hemp-growing regions get one. The cut stalks are left in windrows to dew-ret in the field, then baled and sent for processing.

2–3 harvestswindrowing and baling
Stage 03 of 06

Retting

The stalks are soaked in a large pool lined with a waterproof membrane; pH is monitored with instruments.

Retting in an arid climate. A large basin lined with a waterproof membrane has been built on site for this stage. pH control, water recirculation and treatment, and conversion of organic residues into fertilizer are areas the team is working on (see “Science & R&D”).

pH controlwaterproof membrane
Stage 04 of 06

Decortication

Decortication line capacity: ~3.5–4 t of raw material per hour.

The decortication lines mechanically separate the stalk into two product streams: ~20% bast fiber and ~80% hurd. Line settings are matched to the end use: long fiber of 35 cm and above for supply straight after decortication, and 50–200 mm for fiber intended for cottonization.

~20% fiber~80% hurd
Stage 05 of 06

Deep processing

Degumming and cottonization to a staple length of 30–50 mm.

Degumming removes pectins and lignin, making the fiber softer, lighter and cleaner. Cottonization brings it to a 30–50 mm staple length suitable for blending with cotton on standard spinning equipment. Molded pallets are pressed from hurd on a 2 × 1,600 t line. Start-up of the deep processing lines: October 2026.

Degummingcottonization30–50 mm
Stage 06 of 06

Quality control and export

Batch quality certificate, uniform bales, 40-ft containers. EXW / FCA Tashkent; FOB and CIF by agreement.

Every batch receives a quality certificate with laboratory values (moisture ≤ 12%, impurity content ≤ 15%), a certificate of origin and an electronic phytosanitary certificate with QR verification. The fiber is pressed into uniform bales, ~10.5 t per 40-ft container.

Stalk economics

One stalk — two value streams

After decortication, the stalk is split into two product streams — a zero-waste model in which both generate revenue.

~20%

Bast fiber → textiles

  • Degumming — softer, lighter, cleaner
  • Cottonization — 30–50 mm staple length for blending with cotton
  • Packed in uniform bales, ~10.5 t per 40-ft container
~80%

Hurd → composites

  • Molded pallets — 2 × 1,600 t pressing line, from Q4 2026
  • Board materials — under development
  • Hempcrete, acoustic and thermal insulation
  • Bioenergy
